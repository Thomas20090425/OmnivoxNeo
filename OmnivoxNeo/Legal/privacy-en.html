Privacy Policy

Omnivox Neo talks to your college and, if you choose to send feedback, to nobody else. There is no account to make, no server of ours in the middle, and nothing to opt out of.

Last updated 13 October 2026 · Applies to Omnivox Neo for iPhone, iPad and Mac

Omnivox Neo is an independent app that signs in to your college's own Omnivox portal on your behalf and draws what it finds there in a nicer shape. It is not made by, affiliated with, or endorsed by Dawson College or Skytech Communications, who make Omnivox.

That independence is the thing to understand about privacy here, because it splits the question in two:

  • Your school data belongs to your college and its Omnivox platform. Your grades, your messages, your timetable, your student record — those live on your college's Omnivox servers, and the college's and Skytech's own policies govern them. Nothing in this document changes that. The official policies are linked below.
  • This policy covers only the app on your device — what it asks your phone for, what it writes down locally, and where it sends things. That is the part we are responsible for, and it is a short list.

The short version

Omnivox Neo has no backend. There is no Omnivox Neo server, no account, no sign-up. When you use the app, your device talks straight to your college's Omnivox address over HTTPS — the same server your browser would reach — and the app is simply the thing drawing the result. Nobody sits in between, because there is no in between to sit in.

We never see your password, your grades, your messages or your name, because none of it is ever sent to us. There is nowhere for it to go.

Your college's policies

Because your school data is handled by Omnivox and your college rather than by this app, the policies that actually govern that data are theirs. Read them — they are the ones that matter for your record:

  • Omnivox privacy policy — Skytech Communications The vendor-level policy for the Omnivox platform: the portal, the online services and the official Omnivox Mobile app. En français.
  • Security and Confidentiality — Dawson College Omnivox Dawson's own statement on how your Omnivox session is secured, including its guidance on looking after your password.
  • Usage Conditions — Dawson College Omnivox The terms you accepted for using your college's portal. They apply to your account however you reach it, including through this app.

If your college ever needs something from you before it will let you in — a student ID photo, an agreement to re-read, a password reset — Omnivox Neo cannot resolve that for you. It says so and sends you to your college's own site to deal with it there.

What the app sends, and where

One destination carries everything about you: the web address of the college you picked when you set the app up. For Dawson that is dawsoncollege.omnivox.ca. The app refuses to treat anything else as part of your session — a link has to be HTTPS, on that exact host, on the standard port, or the app hands it to your browser instead of signing in to it.

There is a second address, and it carries nothing about you. Before the app starts, it reads a small file from raw.githubusercontent.com listing which versions of Omnivox Neo are still fit to use — that is how a build with a serious fault can be told to stop. It is an ordinary download of a public file. Nothing is sent with it beyond what any web request discloses: your device's IP address, and the fact that something asked for that file. No identifier, no account, no version report, nothing about you or your college.

A third and a fourth exist only because of the feedback feature, and only when you use it. Opening Feedback and Roadmap reads the project's public issue list from api.github.com — the same list anybody can read in a browser, fetched with no account and nothing identifying attached. Actually sending something adds thomas20090425.github.io, which serves the form, and bugdrop.neonwatty.workers.dev, which receives what you typed and files it. Those two are described in full below, because unlike everything else here they involve a company that is not your college.

What goes to that one destination is what a sign-in needs and what a page request needs: your student number and password at the login screen, your two-factor code if your college asks for one, session cookies afterwards, and ordinary requests for the pages you are looking at. That is identical to what your browser would send. When you write a MIO message in the app, that message goes to your college's server and is delivered by your college's mail system.

There is nothing else. To be specific about the things apps usually do and this one does not:

  • No analytics or usage tracking of any kind. The app does not measure you.
  • No crash or diagnostic reporting service.
  • No advertising, no ad identifier, no profiling, and no sale or sharing of anything.
  • No third-party SDKs. The app itself is built entirely from Apple's own frameworks, and no vendor code is linked into it or runs at launch. The one piece of outside code anywhere near it is the feedback form, which is a web page that only exists while you have that form open — described below.
  • No server operated by us, so no copy of anything on one.
  • Your password and your authenticator secret are never synced to iCloud. The app uses no CloudKit and no iCloud Keychain. The one exception is calendar sync, described below, and it is yours to switch on or leave off.
  • No account with us, no email list, no newsletter.

The app declares this to Apple in its privacy manifest as collecting no data types and performing no tracking, which is why its App Store listing says Data Not Collected. The feedback form does not change that: nothing is gathered from you, the whole of it is what you chose to type into a box, and it is never used to identify you, to measure you, or for advertising of any kind.

One honest caveat about web content

Some of what your portal serves — an announcement with an image in it, a document a teacher uploaded, a link in a MIO message — is web content your college is serving, and the app renders it the way a browser would. If your college's page itself pulls in something hosted elsewhere, that request happens the same way it would in Safari. The app does not add any such content, but it also cannot rewrite what your college publishes. Links you tap that lead off your college's host are opened in your browser rather than inside your session.

Features that are not in Omnivox, and what they will never be

Omnivox Neo already does things the official portal does not — a Home Screen widget, a built-in authenticator, a timetable that works with no signal, calendar sync — and more will be added. Reporting a bug and asking for a feature were listed here as planned; they have now been built, and the section below is the promised account of them.

Those additions change nothing about the promise on this page, and three rules bind every one of them:

  • No feature will be added in order to collect information about you. There is no analytics here, no usage measurement, no profiling, no advertising, and none will be added.
  • If a feature ever needs to send something off your device — a bug report is the obvious example, since a useful one carries a description of what went wrong — it will be off until you turn it on, it will show you what it is about to send before it sends it, and it will send the least that makes it work. Nothing gathered for one purpose will be reused for another.
  • It will be written on this page before it ships, with the date at the top moved, and you will be asked to accept the revised terms. You will not find out afterwards.

Being an unofficial app is not a reason to be vague about this. It is the reason to be exact: no institution stands behind Omnivox Neo, so the only assurance you have is what it tells you about itself, and that has to be true and checkable.

Reporting a bug, or asking for a feature

This is the one part of Omnivox Neo that sends something to somewhere other than your college, and it is the one part that is entirely yours to use or ignore. Nothing here happens unless you open Account → Feedback and Roadmap and choose to do it. Nothing is collected in the background, at any point, by any part of it.

Reading the list

Opening the screen fetches the project's public issue list from GitHub's API at api.github.com. That is an anonymous request for a public page: no account, no key, no identifier, and nothing about you, your device beyond its IP address, or your college. The reply is kept on your device so the screen opens with something on it, and it holds nothing but public issue titles and their status. It is deleted along with everything else when you turn off Instant Open and Offline Access, or delete the app.

Sending something

The form is a web page served from GitHub Pages, shown inside the app in a window of its own. That window is sealed off from your Omnivox session: it has its own storage, created empty when you open it and destroyed when you close it, and it has no access to your cookies, your password, your timetable, your grades or your messages. It cannot read them, and nothing in the app passes them to it.

What is sent is exactly what you type into the form, plus two things you should know about:

  • Your name, only if you type one. The box is optional and may be left empty; leaving it empty is normal and the report is then shown as coming from an anonymous contributor. Whatever you put in it is published with the report, so a first name or a nickname is the sensible thing to use. No email address is asked for — that field is switched off.
  • Your app version, so a report can be matched to the build it came from. That is the whole of what is attached automatically.
  • A screenshot, only if you leave the box ticked. It is never taken automatically, and inside the app it can only photograph the feedback form itself rather than the screen you had the problem on — so it is usually worth unticking. Console logs are switched off and start unticked.

What you write becomes public. It is filed as an issue on the project's GitHub repository, where anyone can read it, and it stays readable there. That is the point — it is how you can see what happened to your report instead of having to trust that something did — but it means the form is not the place for your password, your student number, your grades or anything else you would not put on a public page. The app says so before it opens the form, and the form says so again.

The two companies this involves

Filing an issue on GitHub needs a key that can write to the repository, and a key shipped inside an app is a key everybody has. So the app does not hold one. Instead the form is carried by BugDrop, an open-source service that receives what you typed at bugdrop.neonwatty.workers.dev and files the issue on your behalf. What you submit therefore passes through BugDrop's server and ends up on GitHub's, and both of those are companies with their own policies, which govern what they do with it:

  • BugDrop The service that receives the form and creates the issue. Its code is public.
  • GitHub Privacy Statement GitHub hosts the issue list, the published form, and the repository. Reading the list, loading the form and filing a report are all requests to GitHub.

This is the only place in Omnivox Neo where anything of yours reaches a third party, and it only happens because you filled in a form and pressed Submit. If you never use it, nothing about you goes anywhere except your college.

What stays on your device

This is the full inventory. All of it is inside the app's own storage on your device, and all of it is removable from inside the app.

Your Omnivox password
Only if you ask the app to remember it, so you do not retype it daily.
Device keychain, pinned to this device, behind Face ID or Touch ID
The protection here is enforced by iOS, not by the app's screens: the password is stored so that it is unreadable while the device is locked, cannot leave this device, cannot appear in a backup, and cannot be read at all without a successful biometric check. If someone adds their face or fingerprint to your device, iOS invalidates the stored password and the app has to ask you for it again.
Your authenticator secret
Only if you set up the built-in authenticator, so two-factor codes fill themselves in.
Device keychain, never synced to iCloud
Treat it like any other two-factor secret: it is the thing that generates your codes.
Your session
The cookies and trusted-device state that keep you signed in between launches, exactly as a browser keeps them.
Isolated web session storage, plus the keychain, separately for each account you add
A copy of your timetable
Only when you turn on Instant Open and Offline Access. It is what lets the app open on your schedule straight away, and still show it with no signal.
App's own storage, written with full file protection on iOS and excluded from backups
On iPhone and iPad this file is cryptographically unreadable while your device is locked. Turning the setting off deletes it immediately.
Your grades, documents and messages
Held in memory while you are reading them, and gone when the app closes. Omnivox Neo does not write your grades or your MIO messages to storage.
Memory only — nothing written down
This is why those screens are greyed out in offline mode: there is genuinely no saved copy to show you.
What your Home Screen widget draws
Your name as the portal gives it, your college's name, your accent colour, and today's classes with their times and rooms.
Shared app-group storage, readable by the widget, included in device backups
Worth saying plainly: a widget has to be able to draw itself on a locked device, so this small copy is not behind Face ID, and unlike the file above it is not excluded from your device backup. It holds only what you can already see on your Home Screen. Nothing else is shared with the widget — the widget process has no access to the app's keychain, cookies or anything else. It is removed when you sign out.
Documents you open
A file a teacher posted is downloaded so it can be shown or shared, and lands in the system temporary folder that iOS clears on its own.
Temporary storage
Your own settings
Course colours and icons, a wallpaper you chose, which announcements you have already seen, your language, and whether the biometric lock is on.
App preferences and the app's own storage
Calendar entries, if you ask for them
Deadlines and classes mirrored into a calendar the app creates for you.
Your device calendar, in a calendar named for the app
See the note on calendar access below.

If you would rather it kept nothing

Leave Instant Open and Offline Access off, in Account → On This Device, and don't ask the app to remember your password. You will sign in each time and the app will hold nothing but your session, like a browser tab. The trade is the one you would expect: no instant open, and nothing to show when you have no signal.

What the app asks your device for

Face ID or Touch ID

Used to unlock the saved password and to guard the saved timetable. Your biometric data is never available to the app — iOS performs the check and tells the app only whether it succeeded. The app asks for this only if you turn the lock on.

Calendar

Only if you turn on calendar sync. iOS offers apps either write-only access or full access, and write-only cannot update or remove an entry it previously created — which is the whole job here, since your timetable changes. So the app requests full access, and then uses it narrowly: it creates a calendar of its own, and only ever reads and writes inside that one. It does not list, read, or touch your other calendars or your personal events, and it does not copy anything out of them.

Calendar sync is the one thing that leaves your device

The calendar it creates is made inside whichever account your device already uses for new events. On most iPhones that is iCloud — which is the point, because it is what makes your class times show up on your Mac and iPad too. But it means that with calendar sync on, your course names, your room numbers and your coursework deadlines are uploaded to Apple and stored in your iCloud account, under Apple's privacy policy rather than this one.

That is the only case where anything about your schoolwork goes anywhere other than your college. If you would rather it did not, leave calendar sync off — everything else in the app keeps working — or change your default calendar to On My iPhone before switching it on.

The clipboard, during two-factor setup only

If you use the guided setup for the built-in authenticator, the app watches your clipboard for a few seconds while that screen is open. It has to: Omnivox hands over the authenticator secret by copying it, and there is no other way to catch it. iOS shows you a paste banner when this happens.

It watches only on that screen, it is looking for one thing, and the moment it has that secret it clears your clipboard — so the secret is not left behind for other apps to read, and does not travel to your other devices through Universal Clipboard. Nothing read from the clipboard is stored, logged or sent anywhere.

Nothing else

No location, no contacts, no photo library, no microphone, no camera, no push notifications. The app asks for no permission it does not use.

Deleting your data

Both of these are in the app, under Account:

  • Log out ends the session and removes the saved password, the trusted-device state, every cookie and cached web file for that account, the saved timetable, the widget's copy, and the mirrored calendar. Your name and your enrolled authenticator stay, so signing back in needs only your password.
  • Removing the account from the device does all of that and also deletes the authenticator secret, your read-state, your personalisation, and the account's folder — so the next sign-in starts from nothing.

Deleting the app removes everything it stored, as with any iOS app. One thing an app cannot reach: a calendar you asked for stays in your calendar app until you delete the app, since it was written into your own calendar store — if you want it gone first, turn calendar sync off before deleting.

None of this touches your actual school record. To change or delete anything held by your college, you have to go to your college — this app has no privileged access to your record and no ability to delete anything on their servers.

Demo mode

The app includes a demo account that uses invented students, courses, grades and messages, and never contacts any server. It exists so the app can be shown without exposing a real student's portal. Nothing you do in demo mode leaves your device, because nothing in demo mode goes anywhere.

Students under 14

Omnivox Neo is for students who already hold a college portal account, which in Quebec means CEGEP-age and older. The app does not knowingly collect anything from anyone, of any age, because it does not collect anything at all.

Changes to this policy

If the app's behaviour changes, this page changes with it, and the date at the top moves. Because the app has no server, there is no way to change what it does with your data without shipping you a new version — which means you can always see what you agreed to by reading this page next to the version you are running. The app shows its version under Account.
